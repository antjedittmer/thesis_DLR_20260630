\subsubsection{Results on FAST}\label{subsubsec:qLMPCFASTresults}

Figure~\ref{fig:CtrlCmpFAST} shows the baseline PI controller, the original qLMPC of the first part, and the notch filter and smooth designs of Section~\ref{sec:qLMPCFAST} on FAST v8.16 for the stepped wind sweep and the turbulent 18\,m/s wind field of the first part, with a pitch rate limit of 13\textdegree/s. The standard deviations and loads are summarized in Tables~\ref{tab:CtrlComparisonFASTSweep_PI_beta_flapNotch_flapNotchSmooth12} and~\ref{tab:CtrlComparisonFASTNTM18B_PI_beta_flapNotch_flapNotchSmooth12}, with the ratio of the baseline PI value to the value of the respective controller in parentheses.

In the turbulent wind, the original qLMPC, which performs well on Mdl2, fails on FAST: the rotor speed varies between 10\,rpm and 14.3\,rpm, and the standard deviations of the rotor speed and of the power tracking error are 3.7 and 3.6 times those of the baseline PI controller. The notch filter design reduces these standard deviations by factors of 3.8 and 3.3 compared with the baseline PI controller, and the smooth design by factors of 2.2 and 2.0. This is less than the factor of 11.4 on Mdl2 (Table~\ref{tab:stddev_unified}), but still a considerable smoothing of the power output. In contrast to Mdl2, however, the tower fore-aft acceleration is not reduced but increased, by 35\% for the notch filter design and 16\% for the smooth design, and the tower base DEL increases by 27\% and 12\%. The blade DEL is at most 4\% higher than with the baseline PI controller. The pitch rate standard deviation is 5.9 times that of the baseline PI controller for the notch filter design and 2.4 times for the smooth design.

In the stepped wind sweep, the differences between the controllers are small. The original qLMPC performs about as well as the baseline PI controller. Neither FAST design improves the power tracking, whose standard deviation is 2\% and 10\% higher than with the baseline PI controller, while the tower fore-aft acceleration increases by 48\% and 21\% and the tower base DEL by 5\%.

The adaptations thus make the qLMPC applicable to FAST and retain a large part of the power smoothing in turbulent wind, but not the reduction of the tower loads achieved on Mdl2. The smooth design trades part of the power smoothing for considerably less pitch activity and a smaller increase of the tower loads.
